# Fundamental lens

Purpose: decide whether the business is good, whether the numbers are real,
and whether the price is sane. Cover **everything** — but with judgment: spend
words where this company is unusual, not on boilerplate. Every claim gets a
number; every number gets a source and a period.

Use consolidated financials unless the company is standalone-only; say which
you used. All figures in ₹ crore unless quoting per-share data.

## 1. Growth

- Revenue and PAT CAGR over 3 / 5 / 10 years (10-yr from screener.in). Note
  acceleration/deceleration, not just the average.
- Last 4–6 quarters: YoY and QoQ revenue/EBITDA/PAT — is the current
  trajectory confirming or contradicting the long-term story?
- Growth quality: organic vs acquired, volume vs price-led, one-off items
  (other income spikes, exceptional gains) stripped out.

## 2. Profitability

- Operating margin (OPM) trend 5–10 yrs: level, direction, and volatility.
  Compare to the sector norm from the sectoral lens.
- ROE and ROCE, 5-yr trend. ROCE > cost of capital (~12–13% in India) is the
  bar; sustained 18%+ with growth is compounder territory.
- DuPont sanity check when ROE looks great: is it margins, asset turns, or
  leverage doing the work?

## 3. Balance sheet

- Debt/equity and net debt/EBITDA; interest coverage. Trend matters more than
  level — deleveraging cyclicals and releveraging "stable" companies are both
  stories.
- Working capital cycle: debtor days, inventory days, payable days → cash
  conversion cycle, 5-yr trend. Rising debtor days with rising revenue growth
  is the classic stuffed-channel tell.
- Contingent liabilities vs net worth (from AR notes if findable).
- Capex cycle: CWIP trend, announced capex vs OCF ability to fund it.

## 4. Cash flow quality — where fraud shows first

- CFO vs PAT, cumulative over 5 yrs. Cumulative CFO persistently well below
  cumulative PAT = earnings that don't cash-convert; find out why before
  anything else.
- Free cash flow (CFO − capex) trend and FCF yield at current mcap.
- How growth is funded: internal accruals vs debt vs dilution. Check share
  count over 10 yrs — serial diluters transfer your compounding to new
  shareholders.

## 5. Valuation

- P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA (use the sector-appropriate one: P/B for lenders, P/EV
  for insurers, EV/EBITDA for cyclicals/capex-heavy) — each vs:
  (a) the company's own 5/10-yr median band, and (b) the peer set.
- PEG-style sanity: what growth is the current multiple pricing in, and is
  that plausible against the sector lens conclusions?
- Reverse-engineer expectations: at current price, what does the market
  believe? State it in one sentence ("priced for 20% earnings CAGR for 5
  years") and judge it.
- For cyclicals: refuse peak-earnings P/E; think mid-cycle earnings power.

## 6. Shareholding & ownership signals

- Promoter holding: level + 8-quarter trend. Buying > talking.
- **Pledge**: any promoter pledge gets flagged; >10–15% of promoter holding
  pledged is a standing red flag, especially small caps.
- FII + DII trend over 4–8 quarters; rising retail % with falling
  institutional % in a rallying small cap is distribution — say so.
- Recent insider trades, bulk/block deals, buybacks, and any marquee investor
  entries/exits (note them, don't worship them).

## 7. Management & governance

- Promoter/CEO track record: past ventures, capital-allocation history
  (acquisitions that worked?, dividend/buyback discipline, empire-building).
- Remuneration vs profit (>5% of PAT for promoter-executives deserves
  comment); royalty payouts to parent for MNC subs.
- Related-party transactions of substance; loans/advances to promoter
  entities.
- Auditor: who, tenure, any resignations/qualifications (auditor resignation
  in a small cap ≈ fire alarm).
- Board quality, promoter succession, any SEBI/ED/IT-raid history —
  WebSearch `<company> SEBI order OR investigation OR fraud` takes one minute
  and has saved many portfolios.
- What management promised 2–3 years ago (old concalls/interviews) vs what
  they delivered. Serial overpromisers get a durable discount.

## 8. Red-flag scan (run every time, list only what fires)

- Cumulative CFO ≪ cumulative PAT (see §4)
- Debtor days rising faster than revenue
- High/rising promoter pledge; falling promoter holding into a rally
- Frequent equity raises/warrants to promoters at convenient prices
- Auditor resignation or qualification; frequent CFO exits
- Other income propping PAT; capitalising costs aggressively (CWIP that never
  commissions)
- Contingent liabilities or corporate guarantees large vs net worth
- Acquisition sprees into unrelated businesses
- For SME/microcaps: circuit-to-circuit price action + shallow floating stock

One confirmed structural red flag caps overall conviction at Medium; two cap
it at Low, whatever the growth numbers say. Cheap + flagged = value trap, not
value.

## Output of this lens

A verdict in three parts: **business quality** (great / good / average /
poor), **numbers credibility** (clean / watch-items / suspect), **valuation**
(cheap / fair / expensive vs what you'd pay for this quality) — each carried
by its two or three strongest data points.
