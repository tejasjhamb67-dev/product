# Technical lens

Purpose: the sector and fundamental lenses decide *whether* the stock deserves
concentrated capital; this lens reads what the market currently believes and
helps time entries, adds, and exits. Report what the chart says even when it
disagrees with the fundamental view — the disagreement itself is information.

All readings must be fetched live (Trendlyne / Tickertape / Investing.com /
TradingView — see sources.md). State the as-of timestamp and whether markets
are open.

## 1. Trend structure

- Primary trend (weekly/monthly frame): sequence of higher-highs/higher-lows
  or lower-highs/lower-lows over 1–3 years. Uptrend / downtrend / sideways
  range — name it plainly.
- Intermediate trend (daily frame, ~3–6 months): confirming or diverging from
  primary?
- Position in 52-week range (e.g. "12% below 52w high, 74% above 52w low")
  and distance from all-time high. Multi-year breakout levels and whether
  price is above or below them.

## 2. Moving averages

- Price vs 20 / 50 / 100 / 200 DMA — above/below each, and the slope of the
  50 and 200.
- Alignment: 20>50>200 all rising = established uptrend; price whipsawing
  through a flat 200 = no-trend, technicals deserve less weight in the
  verdict.
- Recent golden/death cross only if it actually happened recently — don't
  narrate ancient crosses.

## 3. Momentum

- RSI (14, daily): value + zone (overbought >70 / oversold <30 / neutral),
  and any divergence vs price at recent swing points.
- MACD: above/below signal and zero line; recent crossovers.
- Rate of change context: returns over 1m / 3m / 6m / 1y — is momentum
  building or fading?

## 4. Volume & delivery character

- Volume on up-moves vs down-moves recently: accumulation (rising price,
  rising volume, rising delivery %) vs distribution vs churn.
- Delivery % vs the stock's own norm — high delivery on breakouts adds
  conviction; low-delivery spikes are traders, not owners.
- Any bulk/block deals coinciding with volume spikes (cross-ref with the
  fundamental lens shareholding section).
- Liquidity check for small caps: average daily traded value. If a
  meaningful position takes many days of volume to build/exit, say so — in a
  concentrated book, illiquidity is risk, not detail.

## 5. Levels

- 2–3 supports and 2–3 resistances that are *structural* (prior swing
  highs/lows, breakout retest zones, high-volume consolidation shelves, the
  200-DMA) — not every pivot a website prints. Round numbers only when the
  market has actually respected them.
- Current price's position relative to those levels: extended above support
  (chase risk) vs sitting on it (defined risk) vs below resistance (supply
  overhead).

## 6. Relative strength — the concentrated-portfolio tiebreaker

- Stock vs NIFTY 50: 3m / 6m / 1y relative performance.
- Stock vs its own sectoral index: leaders outperform their sector index
  before and during sector moves. A fundamentally loved stock that
  persistently lags its own sector index is telling you something — flag it.
- Vs the applicable cap index (NIFTY Midcap 150 / Smallcap 250) for context
  on segment-wide flows.

## Output of this lens

Three lines: **market verdict** (what current trend + participation says the
market believes), **entry character** (extended / constructive / broken —
where price sits relative to structure right now), and **the levels that
matter** (the support that defines risk, the resistance that confirms the
next leg). Feed the thesis-breaker section: which technical event would
invalidate the setup (e.g. "weekly close below the 200-DMA on rising
volume").
